Students — Supervised theses 2021–2025

Two pillars: molecular control (9 students) and chromosome engineering (7 students, below). Control tools developed in the first pillar became instruments for the second.

Pillar I — Molecular Control
MSc · Aug 2021
Sascha Ferraro
Characterization of Programmable Synthetic Transcription Factors for Creating a Non-cooperative Bistable Toggle Switch
Programmable gene circuit design — foundational logic for the synthetic genomics programme
MSc · Oct 2021
Christoph Wagner
Engineering adaptation: Combining Systems Biology, Synthetic Biology and Bioinformatics to control a plant's phenotype
First MSc supervised at HHU Düsseldorf
Lab rotation · Jun 2023
Lars Broeker
Optogenetic regulation of Arabidopsis DELLA genes
Light-inducible control of plant hormone signalling pathways
BSc · Sep 2023
Sebastian Kusnik
Optogenetic Control of Prime Editing and Flowering in Plants
Light-inducible precision genome editing and flowering control in plants
BSc · Nov 2023
Tobias Falkenburg
Optogenetics in Time
Temporal dynamics of light-inducible systems; light control of circadian and developmental processes
MSc · Jun 2024
Armando Teixeira
Quantitative optogenetic control of gene expression and genetics in plants
Closed-loop quantitative control of gene expression with light
MSc · Feb 2025
Francisco Pirichinsky
Optogenetic control of plant metabolic pathways through modulation of gene expression
Light-inducible control of plant metabolic pathway genes
BSc · 2025
Kevin Dietrich
Optogenetic Systems in Plants
Review and characterisation of plant optogenetic systems
MSc · Jun 2025
Benjamin Zinkler
Optogenetic control and orthogonal reconstruction of photoperiodic regulators
Light-inducible control of photoperiodic and flowering time regulators
Pillar II — Plant Synthetic Genomics
BSc · Sep 2023
Luca Weber
Synthetic Genomics: Establishment of Molecular Techniques and Laboratory Infrastructure
Built the experimental infrastructure — sequencing, transformation, large-DNA tools — that all subsequent synthetic genomics work depends on
BSc · Jul 2024
Dejan Andrejic
Establishing a standardized, computationally aided toolkit for neo-centromere synthesis in Physcomitrium patens
Standardised DNA part library and computational tools for synthetic chromosome construction · Co-author, RepTiles preprint 2025
BSc · Dec 2024
Malik Trippensee
Building a programmable CRISPR based system for seeding CENH3 in Physcomitrium patens
Programmable targeting system for directing chromosome identity to synthetic DNA in P. patens
BSc · Jan 2025 ★
Viktoria Petrova
From Reusable Modular Biological Parts to Plant Synthetic Genomics Driven by Biodesign Automation
First author, RepTiles preprint (bioRxiv 2025) — the field's first published methodology paper · Automated large-DNA assembly design platform from community-standard parts
BSc · Jul 2025
Tobias Finkenrath
Studying the recombinogenic capacity of Golden Braid assembled DNA in Saccharomyces cerevisiae and Physcomitrium patens
Large synthetic DNA assembly and stable integration into the P. patens genome — directly preceded the current synthetic chromosome body experiment · Co-author, RepTiles preprint 2025
BSc · Nov 2025
Sarah Otto
Investigating CRISPR-induced CenH3 tagging in Physcomitrium patens
Centromere protein tagging and quantification — confirmed functional lines for the JTF programme
BSc · Dec 2025
Elisaveta Lobkis
Synthetic Chromosome Stability: Establishing a Bottom-up Approach for Synthetic Telomeres
Synthetic telomere strategy for the plant artificial chromosome — closes the last structural component gap in the programme
Field-building significance: Seven theses in the chromosome engineering pillar collectively address every essential structural component — infrastructure, parts library, targeting, assembly, integration, centromere protein quantification, telomere strategy. Three BSc alumni (Andrejic, Finkenrath, Petrova) are co-authors on the RepTiles preprint, with Petrova as first author.
