2023
BSc graduation
Luca Weber — BSc — September 2023
BSc thesis building foundational infrastructure for the programme. Four contributions: (1) Draft Oxford Nanopore genome of P. patens with statistics comparable to the reference genome; (2) DIY PFGE chamber for chromosome-scale DNA visualisation; (3) reliable protoplast isolation protocol — the rate-limiting experimental step for all transformation work; (4) investigation of histone-directed centromeric DNA deoxyadenosine methylation — the epigenetic centromere question that resurfaces in the GreenBITs KTS application three years later. Also explored synthetic DNA as a data storage medium — which appears in GreenBITs WP2. The centromere methylation thread from this BSc thesis in 2023 directly anticipates the synthetic epigenomics programme of 2026.
📍 HHU Düsseldorf · 29 September 2023

2024
BSc graduation
Dejan Andrejic — BSc — June 2024
BSc thesis directly addressing the centromere boot-up problem. Two core contributions: (1) comprehensive random DNA fragment library for neocentromere formation — the physical toolbox for testing whether epigenetic marking drives centromere specification in higher eukaryotes, which became the centromere body substrate in the JTF programme; (2) computational tool for multiplexed genomic targeting of natural P. patens chromosomes, enabling more efficient homologous recombination for inserting long stretches of synthetic random DNA — directly enabling the pSwAP-In iterative integration system. Also continued DIY PFGE chamber development. Abstract framing: "Here we try to build a fully synthetic neo-chromosome with a focus on neocentromere formation from scratch." Subsequently co-authored the RepTiles preprint.
📍 HHU Düsseldorf · June 2024 · Co-author: RepTiles preprint doi.org/10.1101/2025.02.28.640145
Feb
2025
BSc graduation
Tobias Finkenrath — BSc — February 2025
BSc thesis executing the full three-pronged experimental foundation of the 42 kb knock-in experiment. Three projects: (A) Assembled chunk six of the ~100 kb centromere body — 8 × 1.7 kb random DNA mini-chunks into a 20,558 bp plasmid (pUU5194) using Golden Braid in E. coli — the RepTiles pipeline in direct execution. (B) Added pp108 recombination arms, mCherry fluorescence marker, and NptII kanamycin resistance to create the integration fragment for chromosome 20 of P. patens at the Pp108 locus — the direct predecessor of the 42 kb three-fragment knock-in currently in antibiotic selection. (C) Built and tested the TAR cloning vector in S. cerevisiae (ARS1max, histidine selection, VL6-48N strain) for assembly of the full ~100 kb fragment from chunks. Also a co-author on the RepTiles preprint. His thesis makes the mechanistic logic explicit: regional centromeres in plants are epigenetically driven by CENH3, not sequence-defined like yeast — and therefore the dCas9-FUS/FUS-Venus-CENH3 strategy is the correct approach for a non-repetitive synthetic centromere body.
📍 HHU Düsseldorf · February 2025 · Co-author: RepTiles preprint doi.org/10.1101/2025.02.28.640145

2025
BSc graduation
Sarah Otto — BSc — 2025
BSc thesis: CRISPR/Cas9-mediated tagging of PpCENH3 with NanoLUC-3FLAG-10xHis in P. patens. The donor construct — 10×His, 3×FLAG, NanoLUC, Glycine-Serine linker — enables bioluminescence detection and immunoprecipitation of the centromere histone. Cas9 + gRNA induced DSBs via transient transformation, integrated by HDR. Two clones confirmed NanoLUC expression by bioluminescence measurement. This is the exact same NanoLUC fusion approach from the MSB 2025 clock protein quantification paper — now applied to PpCENH3 in the synthetic chromosome chassis. Connects the Edinburgh quantitative biology lineage (absolute protein quantification) directly to the centromere boot-up programme in Düsseldorf.
📍 HHU Düsseldorf · 2025
2025
BSc graduation
Lisa Lobkis — BSc — 2025
BSc thesis: synthetic telomere strategy for the plant artificial chromosome in P. patens. Addresses the second essential chromosomal boundary element after the centromere — telomeres are required for genomic stability of any linear synthetic chromosome. Investigates whether prior findings in chromosome biology are sufficient to build functional artificial telomeres in a plant system. With Sarah Otto's centromere work and the existing centromere body assembly work (Andrejic, Finkenrath), the programme now has BSc theses covering every essential structural component of the synthetic chromosome: centromere body (random DNA) → centromere protein tagging → centromere histone quantification → telomere strategy → chromosome body assembly → integration methodology.

Students — Graduations in Plant Synthetic Genomics

The programme has two pillars that are not separate: plant optogenetics (molecular control layer) and plant synthetic genomics (chromosome engineering layer). The UV-B optogenetic system tested in Arabidopsis by Kusnik became the dose-control mechanism for centromere seeding in moss by Petrova. The synthetic transcription factor logic of Ferraro became the conceptual predecessor of the dCas9-FUS targeting strategy. Falkenburg's CCA1 optogenetic work bridges clock biology directly to the JTF clock modularity hypothesis.

Centromere (multiple theses) + Telomere (Lobkis) + Body assembly (Finkenrath/Petrova) = all essential components covered by BSc theses
Two pillars, one programme: 16 students supervised across two pillars — 9 in plant optogenetics and 7 in plant synthetic genomics — from August 2021 to December 2025. The pillars are not separate: the UV-B optogenetic system developed by Kusnik (September 2023) became the dose-control mechanism for centromere seeding applied by Petrova (January 2025). Falkenburg's CCA1 optogenetic work (November 2023) bridges clock biology to the JTF clock modularity hypothesis. Ferraro's synthetic transcription factor logic (August 2021) preceded and conceptually enabled the dCas9-FUS centromere targeting strategy. Together the 16 theses cover: synthetic TF circuits → optogenetic dose-control → Prime Editing → clock optogenetics → genome infrastructure → DNA part library → CRISPR targeting → assembly methodology → large DNA integration → centromere protein quantification → telomere strategy. This is not coincidence. It is a training programme structured to build, simultaneously, the molecular control layer and the chromosome engineering layer of a complete synthetic genomics platform.
